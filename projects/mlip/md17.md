## Key innovation
The original **small-molecule DFT molecular-dynamics benchmark**: short ab-initio MD trajectories for benzene, aspirin, malonaldehyde, ethanol, toluene, naphthalene, uracil, paracetamol, benzene-ish molecules (10 molecules, ≤ 21 atoms), released alongside the sGDML method. MD17 became the **standard energy/force RMSE testbed** for the MLIP field for roughly half a decade — essentially every interatomic potential paper from 2017–2022 reported numbers on it.

## Limitations / disadvantages
- Molecules are **tiny (≤ 21 atoms)** and sampled narrowly along a **single MD trajectory** each → limited chemical and conformational diversity.
- The DFT force labels contain **numerical noise** (a force RMSE "floor"), later shown to cap achievable error — fixed by rMD17.
- Small training-set regime (hundreds to ~100k frames) that does not exercise data-hungry large models.

## Benchmarks
Task: **energy MAE (kcal/mol)** and **force MAE (kcal/mol/Å)** given 1k–100k training frames. Representative reported numbers: SchNet, DimeNet, PaiNN, NequIP, and MACE all benchmark here; modern equivariant models (MACE) reach very low force MAE on the easier molecules (benzene, malonaldehyde). (Source: Chmiela et al., Sci. Adv. 2017; subsequent model papers.)
